# Build the finder as a filter, not an oracle

You can build a working low-cap crypto finder in zencharts for well under $100 a month. The sources are free on-chain launch streams (PumpPortal, DexScreener, GeckoTerminal), Telegram call channels read by an MTProto user client, and X used sparingly for confirmation, not as a firehose. All of it runs on one small always-on worker outside Vercel, which writes to Postgres and Redis that the Next.js app reads. The research says something less comfortable about what such a tool can do. No peer-reviewed study shows that social mention velocity predicts memecoin returns after slippage, MEV and latency. The strongest studies find that social attention coincides with same-day price spikes and then **reverses**: influencer tweets are followed by negative returns at 10, 30 and 90 days, worst for small caps. Base rates are brutal too: **68.67% of pump.fun tokens stop trading on launch day**, only about 0.2–1.7% graduate, and 82.8% of memecoins that returned more than 100% showed signs of manipulation. The tool that holds up against this evidence works as a filter first. It hard-gates contract, liquidity and holder risk, scores attention by unique authors rather than raw messages, keeps KOL attention (marketing) apart from smart-money wallet flow (alpha), and treats late social spikes as a warning, not an entry. For a developer in Poland, the legal design point is clear. Keep the tool information-only and identical for every viewer. Don't hold keys or route trades, which would trigger MiCA CASP licensing that Poland could not issue as of mid-2026. Avoid named "shill score" profiles of identifiable people, which are the riskiest GDPR and defamation surface. Prices, API terms and the Polish legal situation below are as of October 2026 and move quickly.

## Social attention arrives with the price spike, then reverses

The academic record is consistent: social signals describe what is happening now and forecast mean reversion, not continuation. Maitre, Pugachyov and Weigert built a survivorship-free daily panel of 161 coins (2018–2022). Abnormal Twitter attention, measured as the log change in daily tweet count, rises together with returns on the same day. High-attention coins then underperform: a long-short tercile portfolio earns a next-day alpha of **−0.08% per day, about −29% annualized** ([FoFI 2024](http://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-148-Nikolay-Pugachyov.pdf)). Three details from that paper bear directly on a finder. First, the overreaction comes from ordinary users' ticker tweets, not from projects' official accounts, and it grows when influential users post. Second, the reversal is statistically significant only for coins **above** median market cap. The authors put this down to limits to arbitrage: small caps can't be shorted, so their overreaction isn't corrected quickly. Third, they concede the long-short portfolio can't be implemented, because most coins can't be shorted. Earlier work points the same way. Tweet counts Granger-cause Bitcoin's next-day volume and volatility but not its returns ([Shen, Urquhart & Wang 2019](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/80420/)). Twitter sentiment predicted altcoin returns "for some currencies" only ([Steinert & Herff 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6279012/)). The sign of small-cap reversal flipped between the pre-COVID and COVID periods ([PMC11602072](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11602072/)), which warns that any fitted weight will drift. On the other side, Liu and Tsyvinski find that attention proxies "strongly forecast" crypto returns, but at the aggregate, large-cap level ([RFS 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v34y2021i6p2689-2727..html)).

Influencer evidence is sharper still. Merkley, Pacelli, Piorkowski and Williams studied about 36,000 tweets from 180 crypto influencers covering 1,600+ assets. Prices rise at first, then cumulative returns turn negative at 10, 30 and 90 days, **worst for smaller-cap tokens**, self-described experts and accounts with large followings ([Review of Accounting Studies 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v29y2024i3d10.1007_s11142-024-09838-4.html)). Secondary coverage gives roughly +1.83% the next day, −2.24% at 10 days and −6.53% at 30 days. Those figures come from a summary and were not checked against the paper's tables ([Futurity](https://www.futurity.org/crypto-influencer-advice-investment-3260122)). An industry count, not peer-reviewed, found that **86% of 1,567 memecoins promoted by 377 X influencers lost 90%+ within three months** ([Benzinga/CoinWire](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/cryptocurrency/24/11/42169183/dont-get-fooled-by-the-success-of-dogecoin-shiba-inu-76-of-x-twitter-influencers-promote-d)). Musk's Dogecoin tweets did move price, with an 8.17% abnormal return within 5 minutes peaking at 17.31% within an hour. Across 47 events, though, the effect was significant only for DOGE, not Bitcoin ([Ante, TFSC 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/tefoso/v186y2023ipas0040162522006333.html)). That shows what one giant account can do to one liquid coin. It says nothing about trading KOL calls on illiquid microcaps.

Pump-and-dump research explains why a social scanner tends to buy late. In six months of 2018, researchers counted **4,818 pump signals** on Telegram and Discord across 300+ coins ([Hamrick et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3310307)). Pumps distorted prices by **65%** on average, and most people other than the organizers lost money ([Dhawan & Putniņš, Review of Finance 2023](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/business-and-management/b2bb78b5-1c72-5082-bb5c-ac3c0a5c92dd/new-wolf-in-town-pump-and-dump-manipulation-in-cryptocurrency-markets)). Two windows have been shown to be predictable. Before the announcement, a model predicted target coins from market features and returned up to 60% over about 2.5 months on small positions ([Xu & Livshits, USENIX 2019](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/xu-jiahua)). After the start, a detector flagged pumps **within 25 seconds at 94.5% F1** ([La Morgia et al.](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/2105.00733)). Only the first window pays, and it relied on CEX-era features (Binance/Yobit, BTC pairs) that may not carry over to DEX memecoins. A study of 14,499 Telegram channels found pump signals were tightly synchronized and preceded price by **seconds**, and that the messages read like organic chat ([Politecnico di Torino](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/3014027)). When mentions spike visibly, the insiders have already bought.

The launchpad era makes base rates the main fact to design around. CoinGecko tracked 18.7M pump.fun tokens from January 2024 to June 2026. **68.67% last traded on launch day, about 80% were gone within a day, and only 4.55% lasted more than 90 days** ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/average-lifespan-of-pumpfun-tokens)). Graduation rates depend on window and method: about 0.63% in September 2025 ([Marino et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)), 0.198% in May–June 2026, counting only the first six minutes ([arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02823v3)), and about 1 in 58 over 2025–2026 per Bitquery, which also found **74% of ordinary wallets held less SOL than they spent within two weeks** ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/pump-fun-memecoin-trader-fatigue-token-survival/)). Manipulation is common even among the winners. Of 707 memecoins on four chains that returned more than 100%, **586 (82.8%) showed at least one manipulation signal** ([arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01963v2)).

There is one quantified, positive social feature for launchpads. Pump.fun tokens with a Telegram link in their metadata graduated at **1.485% versus 0.166% without**, an 8.9x lift with a Cox hazard ratio of 5.40. Tokens with Telegram, X and a website graduated at 1.919% versus 0.110% for tokens with none ([arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02823v3)). The authors claim no causality and no tradability. Even with all three channels, about 98% of tokens still fail to graduate, and graduation is not profit. Marino et al. compute a buy-and-hold breakeven of P(graduation) > (vSol/115)². The baseline and most conditional curves stay **below breakeven**, and they ignore the 1.25% curve fee and MEV ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)). Early rug prediction on Solana reaches only modest MCC of about 0.25–0.36 ([arXiv 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20271v1)). The honest summary: any edge lives in the minutes before a social spike becomes visible, which daily-frequency papers can't observe. Social data is most reliably useful for detection, confirmation and as a warning of a local top, not as a buy trigger.

## Telegram and on-chain streams are the cheap firehose; X is a paid confirmation layer

### X became expensive and policy-fragile in 2026

X moved new developers to prepaid pay-per-use in early 2026. Post reads cost **$0.005 each**, user objects $0.010 and recent counts $0.005 per request. Billing is per item returned, and a post fetched twice in one UTC day is billed once ([X docs: pricing](https://docs.x.com/x-api/getting-started/pricing)). The official post-cap page fetched in October 2026 sets the monthly limit at **3 million post reads**. Several third-party guides and a localized version of the docs still say 2 million, so the cap was probably raised at some point ([X docs: post cap](https://docs.x.com/x-api/fundamentals/post-cap.md); [postproxy](https://www.postproxy.dev/blog/x-api-pricing-2026/)). Recent search, full-archive search and filtered stream all count toward the cap and are all available on pay-per-use. X recommends filtered stream over polling for real-time work. Tracking 100,000 cashtag- or contract-address-matching posts a month costs about **$500** officially, and the full 3M cap about $15,000. Basic ($200) and Pro ($5,000) are closed to new signups ([bundle.social](https://bundle.social/blog/x-api-pricing-2026-costs-limits)).

Platform policy is a bigger risk than price. On 15 January 2026 X revoked API access for "InfoFi" apps that paid users to post. Kaito shut down Yaps, Cookie sunset Snaps, and KAITO fell about 15–20% ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/354736/elon-musks-x-bans-access-infofi-crypto-projects-ai-slop-backlash)). The commercial "mindshare" layer you might have bought is now largely gone, and any feature that rewards users for posting invites a ban. Third-party X data is 12–30x cheaper. SocialData charges $0.20 per 1,000 items ([SocialData](https://socialdata.tools/pricing.md)). Apify's Tweet Scraper V2 charges $0.40 per 1,000 ([Apify](https://apify.com/apidojo/tweet-scraper/pricing)). twitterapi.io quotes $0.15 per 1,000 via vendor mirrors ([docs mirror](https://scriptagc.wasmer.app/https_docs_twitterapi_io)). These vendors carry continuity risk: one Apify actor repriced to $3.5 per 1,000 ([Apify Discord](https://discord.apify.com/unlogical-new-pricing-35-per-1000-results-for-twitter-scraper-kT9m91STNsjP)). They also carry ToS exposure, covered in the legal section below.

### Telegram is the earliest social source and the riskiest account to run

Monitoring public call channels you don't administer requires an MTProto user client (Telethon, Pyrogram or GramJS) logged in as a real account. The Bot API only sees chats the bot has been added to. GramJS supports a passive listener (`client.addEventHandler(handler, new NewMessage({chats: [...]}))`) using API credentials from my.telegram.org and a saved string session ([gram.js.org](https://gram.js.org/); [Habr/Selectel](https://habr.com/en/companies/selectel/articles/802629)). The cost is account risk, not money. Telethon's FAQ warns that Telegram's anti-spam measures "have gotten more aggressive since 2023", that VoIP numbers get banned, and that thresholds are unpublished and "free to change". It recommends aged accounts, no request bursts, and respecting `FloodWaitError` ([Telethon FAQ](https://docs.telethon.dev/en/v2/developing/faq.html)). In practice: run one to three aged accounts, join channels slowly, receive updates passively rather than polling, and use the t.me web preview as a slower, logged-off fallback ([Apify Telegram scraper](https://apify.com/makework36/telegram-channel-scraper)). Telegram content also needs adversarial reading. In a 2020 study of 50M+ messages, 56% of Twitter accounts sharing Telegram/Discord invite links were bots or suspended, 93% of invite links posted by bots pointed to pump-and-dump channels, and about 20% of Telegram channels studied were pump or Ponzi channels ([Nizzoli et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2001.10289)).

### The other platforms are gated, slow or closed to commercial use

The remaining sources sort quickly. **Reddit** reportedly requires manual OAuth approval (2–4 weeks) and a separate agreement for commercial use, with roughly 100 queries per minute. Its commercial pricing is unpublished; the commonly quoted figures are unverified vendor claims ([socialcrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/reddit-api-key-limits-alternatives-2026); [redditapis.com](https://www.redditapis.com/blogs/reddit-api-pricing-2026)). It's useful for slower retail sentiment in subreddits like r/CryptoMoonShots, but not for first detection. **Discord** can only be read by a bot invited to each server, and automating user accounts ("self-bots") is forbidden ([Discord Support](https://support.discord.com/hc/articles/115002192352)). Private alpha servers are effectively out of reach without breaking the rules. **Farcaster** is the most open: a self-hosted Snapchain node gives real-time access to all data for under $1,000/month on 16 GB RAM and 1.5–2 TB of disk ([snapchain GitHub](https://github.com/farcasterxyz/snapchain)). Neynar sells the same data per credit, e.g. 50 credits per `/v2/farcaster/casts` call ([Neynar](https://docs.neynar.com/reference/compute-units.md)). Memecoin volume there is low outside Base. **TikTok**'s Research API excludes commercial users entirely ([TikTok](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/research-api-faq)). **YouTube**'s default quota of 10,000 units allows about 100 searches a day ([Google](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/getting-started)). Both are late signals in any case.

Aggregators help with confirmation and ranking, not discovery, because they index tokens only after those tokens gain traction. **LunarCrush** is the most accessible: Individual $90/month, Builder $300 with all endpoints at 100 req/min, Scale $900 ([LunarCrush](https://lunarcrush.com/support/how-much-does-lunarcrush-cost)). **Santiment** starts at $49/month for 5K calls ([Santiment](https://www.santiment.net/pricing)). **The TIE** is enterprise-only ([The TIE](https://thetie.io/solutions/sentiment-api)).

## On-chain data is nearly free and must gate everything social

### Launch streams and DEX aggregators

On Solana, PumpPortal's WebSocket (`wss://pumpportal.fun/api/data`) streams **new tokens and migrations for free**. Per-token and per-wallet trade streams cost 0.01 SOL per 10,000 events, need an API key and a wallet funded with at least 0.02 SOL, and are limited to one connection. Abusive clients get one-hour bans ([PumpPortal](https://pumpportal.fun/data-api/real-time)). Unofficial samples disagree on details such as whether an empty `keys` array means all tokens, so code against the live docs. For multi-chain launchpads (Zora, Clanker, Four.meme, LaunchLab, Bonk) the most unified feed found is Codex's `onLaunchpadTokenEvent` subscription ([Codex](https://docs.codex.io/api-reference/subscriptions/onlaunchpadtokenevent)). The open-source `launchpad-registry` lists program IDs and factory events if you'd rather listen to contracts directly ([GitHub](https://github.com/dynamolabs/launchpad-registry)). As of July 2026, Four.meme leads on BNB Chain and Zora/Clanker on Base ([Coin Bureau](https://coinbureau.com/analysis/best-memecoin-launchpads)).

**DexScreener** is free and keyless. Its official reference documents 60 req/min on the profile, takeover, ads and trending-meta endpoints ([DexScreener API](https://docs.dexscreener.com/api/reference)), and a third-party comparison puts the pair, token and search endpoints at about 300 req/min ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/compare-data-apis)). It has **no "new pairs" endpoint, no candles, no WebSocket and no bonding-curve coverage**. New tokens appear only through paid profiles, boosts and community takeovers, so treat those as "team is spending money" features, not neutral discovery. DexScreener's own Trending Score mixes volume, makers and holders with page visitors, emoji reactions and paid verification, and the weights are secret ([DexScreener: Trending](https://docs.dexscreener.com/trending)). **GeckoTerminal**, now documented under CoinGecko, offers `/onchain/networks/{network}/new_pools` across 200+ networks. The keyless tier allows about 10 calls/min and is explicitly "not suitable for production", and caches refresh every 30–60 s ([CoinGecko new pools](https://docs.coingecko.com/reference/latest-pools-list); [keyless docs](https://docs.coingecko.com/docs/keyless-public-api.md)). Paid plans start around $35/month, though CoinGecko's own pages quote different Demo limits ([CoinGecko plans](https://www.coingecko.com/en/api/acquisition/entry-plans?locale=ja)). **Birdeye** is the main paid all-rounder: holders, first buyers, wallet PnL and bonding-phase coverage. Starter is $99 for 3M compute units, and WebSocket starts around Premium Plus ($250) by its docs ([Birdeye pricing](https://docs.birdeye.so/docs/pricing)). A comparison site lists different entry tiers and WS from $199. That site sells a competing product, so weight it accordingly ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/compare-data-apis)).

### Holders, smart money and security APIs

For raw infrastructure, Helius plans run Free (1M credits), Developer $49, Business $499 and Professional $999. Mainnet gRPC streaming starts at Business ([Helius plans](https://helius.dev/docs/billing/plans)). Helius's own pages disagree on streaming add-on prices ([Helius pricing](https://helius.dev/pricing)), and you won't need them unless you stream the whole chain. For holder concentration, an aggregator is cheaper in engineering time than raw RPC. Moralis exposes Solana holder metrics from $149/month annually ([Moralis](https://docs.moralis.com/data-api/pricing)). SolanaTracker returns top-100 holders and a risk score from €50/month ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/compare-data-apis)).

Smart-money data is where GMGN's taxonomy is worth copying. It tags `renowned` wallets (KOLs, which carry "social/marketing signal… not necessarily alpha") separately from `smart_degen` wallets ("a statistically proven record of profitable trading"). It exposes `smart_degen_count`, `renowned_count`, and a `rug_ratio` in which >0.3 means high risk ([GMGN agent skills docs](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/gmgnai/gmgn-skills); [GMGN Agent API](https://docs.gmgn.ai/index/gmgn-agent-api)). Its third-party rate limits and terms for redistribution are unconfirmed. Nansen and Arkham sell usage-based API access, and secondary sources disagree on Nansen's price ([toolrank](https://toolrank.io/tools/nansen/); [Arkham](https://arkm.com/api)). The robust route is to build your own list. Pull first buyers of tokens that did well (Birdeye or SolanaTracker), score each wallet on realized PnL and repeat early profitable entries, drop wallets linked to the deployer, bundlers or snipers, and watch the survivors through PumpPortal's `subscribeAccountTrade`. This method is an inference from available endpoints, not a published playbook.

For security, **GoPlus** is the broadest machine-readable source. On EVM it returns `is_honeypot`, `buy_tax`/`sell_tax`, `cannot_sell_all`, `owner_change_balance`, `hidden_owner`, `is_proxy`, `slippage_modifiable` and LP holders with lock info ([GoPlus EVM](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/response-details)). On Solana it returns mint, freeze and close authorities, `balance_mutable_authority`, `transfer_hook`, `default_account_state` and LP burn % ([GoPlus Solana](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/solanatokensecurityusingget)). The free tier is reportedly 150K compute units a month ([GoPlus](https://gopluslabs.io/zh/security-api)). **Honeypot.is** simulates a buy and sell on EVM for free, but a pass can be reversed later by owner-set taxes ([QuickNode guide](https://www.quicknode.com/nl/builders-guide/tools/honeypot-is-by-honeypot-scanner)). **Token Sniffer** costs $99/month for 500 lookups a day ([Token Sniffer](https://tokensniffer.readme.io/reference/pricing)). **RugCheck** covers Solana, but its official API docs weren't reachable for this research. Vendor scales differ, so store raw flags and compute your own score instead of averaging vendor scores.

## Design the score as gates, then three independent signal families

### Gate first, because base rates overwhelm any classifier

With more than 95% of launchpad tokens failing, the default answer has to be "reject", and tokens should earn their way past layered gates before any social score is computed. Practitioners use consistent rules of thumb, though none have been validated empirically. Solana Tracker weights freeze authority (7,500) above mint authority (2,500), flags top-10 holders above 15% (5,000) and penalizes unburned or unlocked LP (4,000) ([Solana Tracker](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/token-safety)). Mobula's "healthy" profile is a dev holding under 5% and a top-10 under 30%. It defines a bundler cluster as 3+ wallets making similar-sized launch buys that later consolidate ([Mobula](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers)). Webacy marks bundlers above 20% and snipers above 30% as high risk ([Webacy](https://docs.webacy.com/glossary/snipers-bundlers)). The memecoin manipulation paper supplies rules you can apply mechanically ([arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01963v2)):

| Pattern | Rule |
|---|---|
| Wash trading | Volume up more than 500% in a day with price change under 5% |
| Liquidity-pool price inflation | Price up more than 100% in 24h, at least 90% buys, no more than 10 unique traders |
| Pump-and-dump | RSI above 80, a rise over 50%, volume surge over 500%, then a 30%+ dump |
| Rug | A single-day drop of more than 99% with volume collapsing more than 99% |

The same rules double as backtest labels. Deployer history is a cheap, high-signal feature. On ETH and BSC, **1% of addresses created more than 20% of tokens**, and about 60% of tokens lived less than a day ([Cernera et al., USENIX 2023](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/cernera)). On pump.fun, more than 15,000 launches in one month had a sniper wallet funded directly by the deployer, with an 87% profitable-snipe rate ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)).

A workable gate on Solana hard-fails on any of these: mint or freeze authority not revoked, `balance_mutable_authority`, an untrusted `transfer_hook`, a frozen default account state, top-10 holders above 30% (excluding LP and bonding-curve accounts), `rug_ratio` above 0.3, or a wash-trade or price-inflation pattern in the last 24h. On EVM it fails on a honeypot flag or failed simulated sell, `owner_change_balance`, `hidden_owner`, or an unverified or proxy contract that isn't allow-listed. Two numbers in this design are suggestions to tune against labelled outcomes, not sourced cutoffs: a liquidity floor of $10–20k and a sell-tax ceiling near 10%. Gates must be **re-run on every refresh**, because authorities, taxes, proxies and LP state can change after a token passes.

### Attention, flow and risk are separate features

Every product surveyed splits "trending" into market activity, attention and trust ([DexScreener](https://docs.dexscreener.com/trending); [LunarCrush via PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/lunarcrush/1.0.1)). A homegrown score should keep that structure, with risk applied as a multiplier rather than added as a term. For attention, count **unique authors and unique channels**, not messages. Compute velocity as unique authors per 5 minutes and express it as a robust z-score against the token's own trailing baseline, `z = (v − median_24h) / (1.4826·MAD_24h + ε)`. For brand-new tokens, use a cohort prior by age bucket, and add acceleration as `z_5m − z_15m`. Weight KOLs by their own history: the fraction of first mentions that reached +100% before −50%, shrunk toward a prior and computed only from calls made before the evaluation time. Count cross-platform confirmation only when the agreeing sources don't share a forward or retweet graph. For flow, track distinct smart-money buyers and net USD flow in 15 minutes. One feature is especially informative: the time between the first smart-money buy and the first social mention. It shows whether the buzz followed the buying, which suggests promotion, or led it. Combine the families as `score = σ(β · rank-normalized features) × risk_multiplier`, starting with hand-set weights and fitting β with logistic regression or LightGBM once you have labels. Alert only when at least two independent families agree. These are design proposals from the notes, not validated results.

Entity resolution is where most finders silently fail. Contract addresses often sit inside URLs, so unwrap `dexscreener.com/solana/<pair>`, `pump.fun/coin/<mint>`, Birdeye and GMGN links first. Match Solana with the base58 pattern `[1-9A-HJ-NP-Za-km-z]{32,44}`. Base58 carries no checksum, so decode the match and require exactly 32 bytes, then confirm the mint exists via DexScreener `/tokens/v1/solana/{addr}` ([TokenPocket](https://help.tokenpocket.pro/en/wallet-faq-en/solana-wallet/sol-address); [Solana Ruby Kit](https://www.rubydoc.info/gems/solana-ruby-kit/7.0.0/Solana/Ruby/Kit/Addresses)). Cashtags are ambiguous because many tokens share a ticker. Resolve a cashtag only when a contract address appeared in the same thread or channel recently, and never alert on a cashtag match alone. For sentiment, CryptoBERT is a cheap first pass. It is MIT-licensed, built on BERTweet and trained on 3.2M crypto posts, but its data is from 2018–2022 and predates pump.fun slang ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/ElKulako/cryptobert)). For the top candidates only, a small LLM with structured output (`is_call`, `is_shill`, `is_scam_warning`) is more useful than bullish/bearish polarity, because "dev sold" or "honeypot" warnings carry more information. The research found no benchmark comparing these classifiers on memecoin slang.

### Bot and shill detection has to be home-built

Off-the-shelf bot scoring for X no longer works. Botometer X has run in archival mode since February 2024. It has no records for accounts created after 31 May 2023 and was trained before ChatGPT, so it can't recognize LLM-driven bots ([OSoMe](https://osome.iu.edu/research/blog/introducing-botometer-x)). The useful features come from the coordination literature. One is cashtag piggybacking, where bots co-mention a low-value ticker with major cashtags; up to 71% of suspicious financial-tweet authors were bots ([Cresci et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1804.04406v1)). Others are the share of posts carrying Telegram invite links ([Nizzoli et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2001.10289)), account age, posting synchrony, near-duplicate text clusters, and suspension rate as a lagging label. No validated thresholds for synchrony or duplicate detection turned up, so expect to calibrate them yourself. Paid promotion is the norm, not the exception. ZachXBT published a price sheet for 200+ influencers, and of about 160 who accepted paid deals, **fewer than 5 labelled their posts as ads** ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/368956/zachxbt-says-over-100-crypto-influencers-accepted-promo-deals-without-disclosing-paid-ads)). Score KOLs on forward returns at 1h, 24h, 10d and 30d, their share of calls that later failed gates, the rate at which they delete posts (archive at ingest), and whether their known wallets sell into their own calls. Following Merkley et al., large-following "experts" calling small caps should start with a negative prior.

### Backtest without fooling yourself

The zencharts habit of a pass/fail `score` command fits here, but memecoin data has particular traps. Store every feature vector **point-in-time and append-only**. Never recompute from data that arrived later, such as indexer backfills, holder counts fetched afterwards, or KOL hit rates that include future calls. Keep dead tokens in the universe, because rugged tokens vanish from APIs; 124 tokens were delisted in the manipulation study alone ([arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01963v2)). Use a first-passage label ("+100% before −50%") alongside rug-within-24h. Fill at the price after your real detection latency plus liquidity-dependent slippage. Report precision@K per day against a baseline of random new tokens matched on age and liquidity, using walk-forward splits only. If the finder can't beat that matched baseline after slippage, it shouldn't alert.

## zencharts needs a separate worker and a DEX candle source

The existing repo is a good host for the read side and a poor host for ingestion. zencharts is a stateless Next.js 16 app whose only dependencies are `next`, `react` and `lightweight-charts`. Its route handlers (`app/api/candles/route.ts`, `app/api/series/route.ts`) run as `force-dynamic` Node handlers with an in-memory `Map` cache and 400/404/502 JSON errors. `lib/sources.ts` knows only Binance and Bitstamp and exports a `fetchJson` helper with a 12-second timeout. `lib/instruments.ts` holds a static `INSTRUMENTS` record. The terminal engine in `lib/terminal/` is React-free, and the `funding`, `dvol`, `cone` and `powerlaw` commands share an `on|off|score` convention. There is no database and there are no environment-variable dependencies.

The ingestion side can't live in Vercel Functions. The Telegram MTProto session, the PumpPortal socket and any Helius subscription all need connections that stay open. Functions top out at 300 s on Hobby and 800 s on Pro, with 1,800 s in beta ([Vercel limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations)). Hobby cron runs only once a day, and Pro cron once a minute ([Vercel cron](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/usage-and-pricing)). Don't start the listeners from `instrumentation.ts`'s `register()`. On ephemeral, per-instance serverless you'd get zero or duplicate subscriptions. That pattern only works if the whole app is self-hosted as one long-lived `next start` process.

The proposed layout keeps pure logic shared and I/O separated, all paths under `/home/tomek/workspaces/zencharts`. Shared, unit-testable modules come first. `lib/screener/types.ts` holds `TokenRef`, `Mention`, `FeatureSnapshot`, `ScoreBreakdown` and `Alert`. `lib/social/extract.ts` handles URL unwrapping, the address regexes, base58 32-byte validation and cashtag resolution. `lib/screener/features.ts` computes windowed unique counts, median/MAD z-scores and acceleration, reusing `lib/indicators/stats.ts` where it fits. `lib/screener/filters.ts` holds the hard gates and the wash-trade and price-inflation rules, `lib/screener/score.ts` the rank-normalized score and breakdown, and `lib/screener/backtest.ts` the point-in-time labels and precision@K, in the style of `lib/backtest.ts`. Adapters reuse `fetchJson`, extracted to `lib/http.ts` if convenient. They are `lib/onchain/dexscreener.ts` (with a 60 rpm token bucket), `lib/onchain/pumpportal.ts`, `lib/onchain/birdeye.ts`, `lib/social/telegram.ts` (GramJS), a budgeted `lib/social/x.ts` that queries only top-K candidates, `lib/social/classify.ts`, and `lib/db.ts` for Postgres plus Upstash Redis.

A new top-level `worker/index.ts` starts the listeners, a one-minute feature-and-score tick and a Telegram Bot API alerter. It runs via `tsx worker/index.ts` on Fly.io, Railway or a small VPS at roughly $5–20/month. Postgres tables cover `messages`, `mentions`, `tokens`, append-only `token_snapshots`, `wallets`, `wallet_trades`, `scores` and `alerts`. Redis holds sliding windows and HyperLogLog unique-author counters. ClickHouse is warranted only if you ingest every pump.fun trade. Next.js stays read-only: `app/api/screener/route.ts`, `app/api/token/[chain]/[address]/route.ts`, and a candles route for found tokens, all `force-dynamic` with the existing error conventions. The bundled Next.js 16 docs note that with Cache Components, GET handlers run at request time and `'use cache'` must sit in an extracted helper, not the handler body. Read `node_modules/next/dist/docs/` before writing these, as the repo's AGENTS.md instructs. Terminal commands should follow the house convention. `scan [chain] [n]` prints the ranked table and `token <ca>` prints the breakdown and switches the chart. `watch <ca>|on|off` toggles a mention-velocity histogram pane with markers at first mention and alert time. `scan score` prints walk-forward precision@K against the random-new-token baseline, mirroring `funding score`. `alerts` lists recent alerts. Secrets go in the already-gitignored `.env.local`: `DATABASE_URL`, Upstash keys, `TG_API_ID`, `TG_API_HASH`, `TG_SESSION`, `TG_BOT_TOKEN`, `TG_ALERT_CHAT_ID`, `BIRDEYE_API_KEY`, `HELIUS_API_KEY`, and optionally `X_BEARER`.

The real blocker is charting. Found tokens have no Binance or Bitstamp listing, so `Instrument` needs to become a discriminated union (`{kind: "cex", …}` or `{kind: "dex", chain, address, pair}`), or the app needs a dynamic `sol:<mint>` resolver, and `getHistory` needs a DEX OHLCV source. DexScreener has no candles. GeckoTerminal, Birdeye and Codex are the obvious candidates, but their OHLCV endpoints and limits weren't verified in this research. `components/Chart.tsx` and `lib/config/overlays.ts` also need a closer read before you add a pane. On costs, a single-user setup runs about $5–20 for the worker, $0–25 for Neon or Supabase, $0–10 for Upstash and $0 for DexScreener and PumpPortal. LLM classification of the top ~200 messages an hour costs single-digit dollars a day. X is optional and runs about $300/month at 2,000 reads a day.

## Stay information-only: GDPR and MiCA market abuse are the live EU risks

This section summarizes sources and is not legal advice. Data collection splits cleanly on whether you log in. In the US, logged-off scraping of public data survived challenges in **Meta v. Bright Data** (January 2024) ([Farella Braun](https://www.fbm.com/technology/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/)) and **X v. Bright Data** (May 2024), where contract claims over public posts were held preempted by copyright law ([Skadden](https://skadden.com/insights/publications/2024/05/district-court-adopts-broad-view)). X was later allowed to amend its complaint, and its current status is unverified ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/401510/x-corp-can-revive-scraping-battle-with-bright-dat.html)). Logged-in scraping and fake accounts remain enforceable contract breaches: hiQ ended with a $500,000 consent judgment and an injunction ([Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2022/12/linkedins-data-scraping-battle-with-hiq-labs-ends-with-proposed-judgment/)). X's terms since November 2024 add liquidated damages for accessing more than 1M posts in 24 hours, €15,000 in the EU version, reaching anyone who "induces or knowingly facilitates" a breach. Sources disagree on whether that amount is flat or per million posts, and enforceability is untested ([Knight Institute](https://knightcolumbia.org/content/knight-institute-says-xs-new-terms-of-service-will-stifle-independent-research); [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/x/x-terms-of-service/liquidated-damages-for-excessive-post-access-eu/)). Discord bans scraping and self-bots and enforced this against Spy.pet ([Discord developer policy](https://support-dev.discord.com/hc/articles/25279999805975)). Telegram user-client monitoring sits in a grey zone: the research found no Telegram term that addresses it directly, and the practical sanction is account bans. These US rulings don't bind EU courts and say nothing about GDPR.

For a developer in Poland, GDPR is the binding constraint. Public posts are personal data. Legitimate interest is the only realistic lawful basis, and it requires a documented three-part test. The EDPB's draft Guidelines 03/2026 on scraping, adopted for consultation in July 2026 and not final, call scraping "large-scale processing". They recommend excluding sites that signal opposition (robots.txt, login walls), pseudonymizing, deleting early, and publishing an Art. 14 notice ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_2020603_webscraping_v1_en_0.pdf)). The Dutch DPA goes further: commercial scraping of personal data is "almost always" unlawful, though that position is reportedly under review ([Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/dutch-web-scraping-guidance-warn-businesses-gdpr-breach-risk)). The practical line is between counting and profiling. Aggregate mention counts with pseudonymized authors are easy to defend. Named KOL profiles with shill scores and "rug history" are Art. 4(4) profiling that probably needs a DPIA, triggers objection rights, and adds defamation risk if you label a person a scammer. If you build KOL scoring, keep it internal, keyed by hashed handle, with short retention and an objection channel.

Financial regulation draws a similar line between publishing and acting. ESMA's June 2026 Q&A says MiCA's advice perimeter is **broader than MiFID II's** and turns on personalized recommendations issued "otherwise than exclusively to the public" ([ESMA Q&A 2882](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-data/questions-answers/2882)). A leaderboard identical for every viewer, with no suitability framing, is probably outside it. Per-user alerts tuned to a portfolio and framed as "buy" move toward licensable advice. The sharper exposure is market abuse. **MiCA Art. 91 applies to "any person"** and covers spreading misleading information or rumours, as well as voicing an opinion on a crypto-asset after taking a position without disclosing it ([ESMA Single Rulebook](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-91-prohibition-market-manipulation)). A developer who buys tokens before publishing alerts about them fits that description. Adopt a written personal-trading policy (no holdings in listed tokens, or a blackout), disclose any conflicts, label any paid placement, and give every user the same delivery time. The notes flag MiCA's application to DEX-only tokens never admitted to a CASP platform as unverified. Fraud and Polish criminal law apply either way. In the US, the SEC staff said in February 2025 that typical meme coins are not securities ([Winston & Strawn](https://www.winston.com/en/blogs-and-podcasts/non-fungible-insights-blockchain-decrypted/sec-staff-under-new-administration-issues-statement-declaring-that-meme-coins-are-not-securities)). Fraud remains prosecutable, as Operation Token Mirrors showed when the FBI minted its own token to catch wash-trading market makers ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/285503/feds-charge-gotbit-others-market-manipulation)).

Execution is the bright line. Routing user orders, executing them as agent, or running auto-buys and copy-trading under a mandate map onto MiCA CASP services (reception and transmission, execution, portfolio management), judged by actual order flow rather than labels ([ESMA Q&A](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/221381)). Poland's transitional window closed on **1 July 2026** with no designated competent authority ([KNF](https://nuk.knf.gov.pl/knf/en/komponenty/img/Statement_of_the_Polish_Financial_Supervision_Authority_97112.pdf)). President Nawrocki vetoed the implementing act for a third time in June 2026 ([Polsat News](https://www.polsatnews.pl/wiadomosc/2026-06-11/trzecie-weto-w-sprawie-kryptoaktywow-decyzja-karola-nawrockiego/)), and Polish firms were reported to be seeking licences abroad ([XYZ.pl](https://xyz.pl/rynek-kryptoaktywow-przegrywa-z-polityka-nie-znam-firmy-ktora-nie-stara-sie-o-zagraniczna-licencje/)). The status after June 2026 couldn't be confirmed, so check sejm.gov.pl and Dziennik Ustaw before anything commercial. Holding keys also carries operational risk. The Banana Gun Telegram bot was drained of about $3M from 11 users in September 2024 through a message-oracle bug ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/317282/banana-gun-telegram-bot)).

## Conclusion

The research reverses the usual framing of this project. The valuable output of a social-signal finder isn't a list of coins to buy. It's a fast, cheap way to reject the roughly 99% of launches that fail and to flag the moment a token's attention has turned into exit liquidity. The evidence on direction is consistent: attention reverses, KOL calls reverse hardest in small caps, and most big winners were manipulated. So the defensible edge, if one exists, sits in a narrow band. It's the minutes when on-chain smart-money flow and early, unsynchronized, unique-author chatter agree before a visible spike, filtered through strict contract and distribution gates. That hypothesis has not been tested in the published literature. Your own point-in-time snapshot store is therefore the most valuable thing to build first, before any alert goes out, and `scan score` against a matched random-token baseline is the gate the whole project should have to pass.

The cost structure also changed in 2026 in a way that favours a solo builder. X's pay-per-use pricing and the InfoFi ban dismantled the commercial mindshare products, while launch streams, DEX snapshots and security checks stayed free or nearly free. That makes Telegram plus on-chain the natural core and X an optional confirmation layer. The legal design mirrors the statistical one. A public, identical, information-only screener with pseudonymized social data and a personal no-trade policy stays on the safe side of GDPR, MiCA advice and Art. 91. Adding personalization, named influencer profiles, auto-trading or self-dealing each moves the tool into territory that a Polish developer, without a domestic licensing route, can't currently enter cleanly.

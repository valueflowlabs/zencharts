# Build a migration-ready wallet, because quantum-safe Bitcoin does not exist yet

As of October 2026 **no wallet can make bitcoin on L1 quantum-safe, because Bitcoin consensus has no post-quantum signature check**. BIP-360 (now "Pay-to-Merkle-Root", P2MR) was merged into the BIPs repository as a Draft in February 2026, but it adds no PQ algorithm and no opcode, Bitcoin Core has merged no PQ consensus code, and no activation is scheduled. What a developer can build today is a **migration-ready wallet**. It keeps coins in never-reused hash-committed outputs (P2WPKH/P2WSH, not P2TR), treats xpubs and descriptors as secrets, keeps every coin on a standard BIP-32 path so a future zero-knowledge seed proof can rescue it, and ships a PQ key-derivation, PSBT and hardware pipeline that can be pointed at whatever output type and opcode a soft fork finally defines. The stakes are concrete. Shor's algorithm breaks both ECDSA and Schnorr on secp256k1, **about 6–7M BTC (roughly 30–35% of supply) already sits behind an exposed public key**, and Google's March 2026 estimate cut the attack to **fewer than 500K physical qubits and about 9 minutes per key after precomputation**, close enough to the 10-minute block interval that even ordinary spends become targets for a fast machine. Expert and government anchors put a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC) at roughly 2030–2035, with a tail into the late 2020s. Developer momentum points to a **hash-based signature opcode (the SHRINCS/SPHINCS+ family) inside a Taproot-like output with no key path**, at a cost of about 2–19x today's block space per transaction unless the witness-weight rules change. Claims marked *secondary* below come from press or vendor coverage rather than primary documents, and claims marked *inference* are the research notes' own reasoning rather than sourced statements.

## Shor breaks every Bitcoin signature, but only where a public key is visible

A CRQC running Shor's algorithm derives the private key from any secp256k1 public key, so it forges both ECDSA and BIP-340 Schnorr signatures; Schnorr adds no quantum resistance because it rests on the same discrete-log problem ([Chaincode Labs, May 2025](https://chaincode.com/bitcoin-post-quantum.pdf)). Grover's algorithm only square-roots the cost of attacking SHA-256 and RIPEMD-160 and parallelizes poorly, so **address hashes and proof-of-work stay safe**. Google's 2026 paper calls hash inversion "not a credible threat" ([arXiv:2603.28846](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28846)). The quantum problem in Bitcoin is therefore entirely a question of where public keys are visible.

**P2PK, bare multisig and P2TR expose a key in the output itself and are vulnerable "from the moment they receive funds"**, while P2PKH, P2WPKH, P2SH and P2WSH reveal the key only when spent ([Chaincode](https://chaincode.com/bitcoin-post-quantum.pdf)). A Taproot key-path output publishes its tweaked key on-chain, which Google's authors call "a security regression" ([arXiv:2603.28846](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28846)). Hash protection is lost through address reuse, through spends of the same coins on fork chains such as Bitcoin Cash, through multisig co-signers and Lightning counterparties who know each other's keys, and through shared xpubs. A CRQC holding an xpub and chain code can compute every non-hardened child public key and then every matching private key, so **one leaked xpub exposes every non-hardened address beneath it**; hardened children stay safe ([Chaincode](https://chaincode.com/bitcoin-post-quantum.pdf)).

The window during a spend has also narrowed. A primed superconducting CRQC has "slightly less than 41%" probability of deriving a key before a Bitcoin transaction confirms, under idealized assumptions that favor the attacker. Fast-clock machines (superconducting, photonic, silicon-spin) threaten mempool spends, while slow-clock machines (neutral-atom, ion-trap) threaten keys already at rest well before they threaten spends ([arXiv:2603.28846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28846)). The notes infer from this that **the first CRQC could threaten ordinary spends, not only dormant P2PK coins**, unless transactions go privately to miners.

| Exposure estimate | BTC | Date and source |
|---|---|---|

The figures agree on a structure. Roughly **1.7M BTC in P2PK forms a hard core** whose owners are probably absent and cannot migrate. **Most of the remaining ~4–5M is reuse-exposed**, much of it on live exchange and custodial addresses that can move quickly once a safe destination exists. Taproot exposure is small by value but large by UTXO count (*inference* from the cited figures). CoinShares' counter-view that only about 10,200 BTC sits in outputs large enough to move markets is reported only *secondhand* ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/chaincode-labs-sizes-up-the-quantum-threat-to-bitcoin/)). The notes argue that Google's minutes-per-key figure weakens its "too slow to attack many small UTXOs" premise, though no published rebuttal says so.

## Algorithmic progress has pulled the threat into the 2030–2035 window

The cost of breaking 256-bit elliptic-curve keys has fallen faster than hardware has improved. In 2017 the estimate was **about 2,330 logical qubits and 1.26×10^11 Toffoli gates** ([arXiv:1706.06752](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06752), cited from prior knowledge, not re-fetched). In 2022 it was **about 13M physical qubits for a one-day attack** ([AVS Quantum Science](https://doi.org/10.1116/5.0073075)). In March 2026 Google Quantum AI published two circuits: **under 1,200 logical qubits with under 90M Toffolis, or under 1,450 with under 70M**. These need **fewer than 500K superconducting physical qubits**, run in about 18–23 minutes, and leave **about 9 minutes of work once the key is revealed**. The circuits themselves were withheld and validated by a zero-knowledge proof ([arXiv:2603.28846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28846); [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/safeguarding-cryptocurrency-by-disclosing-quantum-vulnerabilities-responsibly/)). The same week, Oratomic, Caltech and Berkeley argued that **about 26,000 reconfigurable neutral-atom qubits could compute P-256 discrete logs "in a few days"**, with 10,000 qubits as a lower bound ([arXiv:2603.28627](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.28627)). A July 2026 Chinese preprint brought secp256k1 down to **835 logical qubits**, at about 20x more Toffolis than Google's circuit (*secondary* coverage, [PostQuantum.com](https://postquantum.com/security-pqc/ecc-secp256k1-835-logical-qubits/)).

Hardware remains one to two orders of magnitude short. IBM and the University of Chicago ran **70 logical qubits through 468 logical T gates** in July 2026 ([IBM](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-07-30-ibm-and-the-university-of-chicago-demonstrate-quantum-advantage,-establishing-trusted-quantum-computation-on-logical-circuits)), and Infleqtion entangled 30 logical qubits in September ([Infleqtion](https://infleqtion.com/infleqtion-achieves-30-entangled-logical-qubits-on-its-sqale-quantum-computer/)). The largest public quantum ECC break, which won Project Eleven's 1 BTC prize in April 2026, was **a 15-bit key** (*secondary*, [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/04/24/project-eleven-q-day-prize-quantum-ecc-attack/)). The notes conclude that **the binding constraint is now sustained fault-tolerant depth (~10^8 Toffolis), not qubit count**. Demonstrated logical T-gate counts are roughly 10^5 short of the requirement, and IBM's roadmap puts ~2,000-logical-qubit machines at 2033 or later (*secondary* trackers, [qrlhub](https://qrlhub.com/en/qubits)).

Institutional timelines cluster around 2030–2035. Chaincode says experts expect CRQCs "between 2030-2035" ([Chaincode](https://chaincode.com/bitcoin-post-quantum.pdf)). The Global Risk Institute's 2025 survey, published March 2026, puts a CRQC at **28–49% likely within 10 years and 51–70% within 15** ([GRI](https://globalriskinstitute.org/publication/quantum-threat-timeline-report-2025b/)). NIST's draft IR 8547 would deprecate ECDSA after 2030 and disallow it after 2035 ([NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8547/ipd)), and Google references its own 2029 migration deadline ([Google](https://research.google/blog/safeguarding-cryptocurrency-by-disclosing-quantum-vulnerabilities-responsibly/)). Bitcoin voices diverge widely. Adam Back said "probably not for 20–40 years" in November 2025, before the March resource cuts ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitcoin-quantum-threat-decades-post-quantum-migration)), while investor Charles Edwards argues for 2–9 years (*secondary*, [dig.watch](https://dig.watch/updates/quantum-computing-threatens-bitcoin-experts-debate-timeline)). For a wallet developer, the relevant comparison is lead time. If a soft fork needs years to specify and activate, and BIP-361's own migration phases run three to five years after activation, **work that starts in 2026 is not early**.

## Protocol proposals converge on a hash-based opcode in a keyless Taproot

**BIP-360 has shrunk into "Taproot minus the key path."** Version 0.12.1 (July 2026) defines a SegWit v2 output (`bc1z…`) whose 32-byte witness program is the Merkle root of tapscript leaves. It has no internal key and no tweak, spends follow BIP-341 script-path rules, and **depth-zero single-leaf trees are anyone-can-spend** ([BIP-360](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0360.mediawiki)). It specifies no PQ signature algorithm. ML-DSA and SLH-DSA appear only as examples of future work through OP_SUCCESSx upgrades, so P2MR protects funds only once a PQ opcode is added or EC spending is disabled. A depth-1 P2MR witness is 135 B, against 66 B for a P2TR key-path spend, and a regtest prototype needed about 96 lines of consensus changes ([Optech, Aug 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/08/07/)). Its merge into the BIPs repository in mid-February 2026 is documentation, not activation; there is **no Bitcoin Core implementation** ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/10/quantum-safe-bitcoin-now-possible-without-a-soft-fork-but-costs-usd200-a-pop)). Pieter Wuille's Delving thread now compares P2MR with **P2TRv2** (which keeps EC key spends for today's fees and adds a hash-based PQ opcode, with EC to be disabled later), P2TRH, and P2QR ([Optech, Aug 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/08/07/)). Wuille later noted that **neither P2TRv2 nor P2MR is categorically quantum-secure**, because both depend on a later decision to stop accepting secp256k1 signatures ([Optech, Oct 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/02/)).

**Migration and sunset proposals remain the contested half.** BIP-361 (Lopp and five co-authors, Draft) would **block sends to quantum-vulnerable outputs about 3 years after activation (Phase A), then invalidate ECDSA/Schnorr spends without a quantum-safe rescue proof about 5 years after (Phase B)**. It names ZK-STARK and commit/reveal rescues without finalizing either, offers **no rescue for P2PK**, and depends on a "TBD Post Quantum Signature BIP" ([BIP-361](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0361.mediawiki)). QRAMP (February 2025) proposed a hard migration deadline and drew objections that it would cause financial harm ([bitcoin-dev](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/8PM6iZCeDMc)). Hourglass V2 would throttle P2PK spends to about one input per block instead of freezing them, which critics say means roughly 32 years to drain ~1.7M BTC plus a fee race ([Delving](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/hourglass-v2-update/2246)). Dan Robinson's PACTs would let owners timestamp salted BIP-322 ownership proofs now and prove them later with a STARK ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/05/02/new-bitcoin-quantum-proposal-offers-satoshi-nakamoto-a-way-to-prove-control-without-moving-btc)). Charles Hoskinson called BIP-361 a hard fork in disguise (*secondary*, [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/04/16/cardano-s-hoskinson-says-bitcoin-s-quantum-fix-is-a-hard-fork-that-can-t-save-satoshi-s-coins)). Antoine Poinsot argued that disabling vulnerable spends should be decided separately from enabling PQ migration, and participants converged on **adding PQ signing to tapscript plus a plain PQ output type** ([Optech, May 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/05/01/)).

**Recovery research makes the hardened BIP-32 tree the asset to protect.** Tim Ruffing proved that **Taproot's script-tree commitment is binding and hiding against quantum adversaries**, so P2TR is PQ-secure if restricted to script-path spends ([ePrint 2025/1307](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1307)). Wuille proposed two triggers for disabling EC spends: a **tripwire** that fires once someone spends a `<NUMS> OP_CHECKSIG` output (proving secp256k1 is broken), and a long-window **miner-signaled lockdown** ([Optech, Jul 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/07/03/)). For coins that never upgrade, the prototypes are as follows.

- **Osuntokun's zk-STARK** proves a BIP-86 output derives from a BIP-32 seed: about 55 s to prove and a 1.7 MB proof, or about 200 KB with recursion ([bitcoin-dev](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/Q06piCEJhkI/m/6BN8qE3gAQAJ)).
- **Project Eleven's Binius prototype** proves in 243 ms. It is unaudited, rooted at the coin-type key, and does not support Taproot ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/07/19/bitcoin-s-quantum-problem-gets-a-recovery-tool-but-not-for-satoshi-s-1-1-million-coin)).
- **Conduition's DropKick** is a commit/reveal design with aggregator batching ([Optech, Sep 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/09/04/)).

All of them require a future soft fork, and **none can rescue pre-HD keys, imported WIF keys, brainwallets or P2PK**.

**Script-only schemes that work today are emergency tools.** StarkWare's QSB gets hash-based security in legacy script at **$75–$200 of GPU work per transaction**. It needs miner-direct submission, cannot be used with Lightning, and its author calls it a "last resort" ([StarkWare](https://starkware.co/blog/quantum-safe-bitcoin-transactions-without-softforks/); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/10/quantum-safe-bitcoin-now-possible-without-a-soft-fork-but-costs-usd200-a-pop)). Lamport and Winternitz prototypes need OP_CAT, which is not active ([Optech topic](https://bitcoinops.org/en/topics/quantum-resistance/)).

**The soft-fork debate in October 2026 has a working direction but no activation plan.** Bitcoin Core has merged no PQ consensus code and no activation parameters exist ([Presidio](https://presidiobtc.github.io/bitcoin-quantum/)). Wuille's position is to **deploy P2TRv2 now for casual users and P2MR for sophisticated users**, with a later P2MR-based type using a new witness style. He argues that wallet and custodian support, not fees, is the bottleneck, and opposes bundling cross-input signature aggregation (CISA); Adam Gibson agrees ([Optech, Aug 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/08/07/); [Optech, Sep 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/09/04/)). Conduition sees P2TRv2 plus CISA as a strong migration incentive and proposes shipping both types. Riard accepts P2TRv2 only if its vulnerable paths can be disabled within the type. Poinsot warns that P2TRv2 and CISA pull in opposite directions and that distinct output types fingerprint their users ([Optech, Oct 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/02/)). The notes infer that the first fork would most likely bundle a PQ output type, a hash-based opcode and possibly a tripwire, that any freeze would be a separate and far more contested fork, and that **activation in 2026–2027 looks unlikely absent an emergency** (*inference*).

## Signature choice is a block-space decision, and hash-based schemes lead

Every standardized PQ signature is 10–270x larger than a 64-byte Schnorr signature. ML-DSA (FIPS 204) and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) were finalized in August 2024, and FN-DSA/Falcon (FIPS 206) was still a draft in 2026 ([FIPS 204](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final); [FIPS 205](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/205/final)). Bitcoin-tailored hash-based designs narrow the gap. Kudinov and Nick's optimized SPHINCS+ variants (SPHINCS+C, TL-WOTS-TW, PORS+FP) reach **about 4,036 B at a 2^40 signature cap** ([ePrint 2025/2203](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2203)). Blockstream's hardware benchmarks use a plainer SPHINCS+ capped at 2^40 at 5,776 B, which is a different construction rather than a contradiction ([Blockstream Research](https://blog.blockstream.com/hardware-wallets-post-quantum-signatures/)). **SHRINCS** combines a stateful XMSS-style path with a stateless SPHINCS+ fallback. Jonas Nick's February 2026 parameters started at 324 B ([Optech, Feb 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/02/06/)). The **September 2026 draft BIP**, the current reference, specifies a 48-B public key, **stateful signatures from 548 B**, a **5,777-B stateless fallback**, and verification claimed at 4–16x faster per byte than BIP-340 ([Optech, Sep 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/09/04/)). Conduition argues for hash-based schemes on weaker assumptions, simpler implementation and fast verification ([Optech, Jul 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/07/03/)). Falcon offers about 20x ECDSA size and faster verification, but constant-time signing is hard and the proposed deployment was effectively a hard fork ([Optech, Feb 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/02/06/)).

The table below is **the notes' own model, not a published figure**. It assumes a 1-input/2-output transaction spent through a P2MR leaf at depth 0 or 1, about 380 WU of non-witness data, and **today's weight rules with no new PQ witness discount**. Signature and key sizes come from the sources above; Falcon-512 sizes are the commonly cited specification figures, not a fetched NIST final. A SQIsign row was dropped because its figures were unverified.

| Scheme | Signature / public key (B) | ≈ tx size (vB) | ≈ tx per block | Cost vs today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Schnorr, P2TR key path (today) | 64 / 32 (in output) | 112 | ~8,970 | 1x |
| SHRINCS stateful (draft BIP) | 548 / 48 | 254 | ~3,900 | 2.3x |
| Falcon-512 | ~666 / ~897 | 496 | ~2,000 | 4.4x |
| ML-DSA-44 | 2,420 / 1,312 | 1,038 | ~960 | 9.3x |
| SPHINCS+ (Kudinov/Nick, 2^40) | 4,036 / 32 | 1,122 | ~890 | 10x |
| ML-DSA-65 | 3,309 / 1,952 | 1,420 | ~700 | 12.7x |

| SLH-DSA-128s | 7,856 / 32 | 2,077 | ~480 | 18.6x |

On this model, a chain of identical 1-in/2-out spends under current rules falls from about 15 transactions per second with Taproot key-path spends (~8,970 per 10-minute block) to **about 6.5 with SHRINCS stateful signatures, and to roughly 0.8–1.6 with ML-DSA, SPHINCS+ or SLH-DSA signatures**. Real blocks carry larger transactions, so absolute rates are lower; the ratios are what matter. That is why the debate has moved away from simply enlarging the witness discount. Antoine Riard estimates a 5,777-B signature costs about 90x today's on-chain fee without a discount. Wuille expects post-Q-day hash-based signatures to need **a costing rule that weights CPU over bytes**. Conduition warns that an 8x larger serialized block would push archival storage into terabytes per year ([Optech, Sep 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/09/04/)). Wuille has floated **segwit "witness styles"**, each with its own weight function ([Optech, Aug 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/08/07/)). Aggregation is still research: STARK aggregation of 512 SPHINCS signatures shrinks 3.8 MiB to a 454 KiB proof but takes about 3 s per signature to prove on an RTX 5090 ([Optech, Jul 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/07/03/)). The wallet consequence is that **fee estimation and coin selection must price each input by its spend type**, and that hash-based public keys of 32–48 B fit a P2MR leaf far better than lattice keys of 1–2 KB.

## Before any soft fork, a wallet's real defenses are hygiene and recoverability

The single strongest defense available today is the one Chaincode recommends: **move vulnerable funds to never-reused P2PKH, P2SH, P2WPKH or P2WSH outputs, and stop address reuse** ([Chaincode, Appendix III](https://chaincode.com/bitcoin-post-quantum.pdf)). For a wallet developer that becomes a default policy. Long-term storage should use P2WPKH (single-sig) or P2WSH (multisig). The wallet should refuse or loudly warn on receive-address reuse, and fully sweep when spending so no change is left behind a revealed key. The notes add a multisig caveat: spending a P2WSH multisig reveals **every** cosigner's key for that address, so fresh addresses matter as much in collaborative custody as in single-sig (*inference*). The cost is a trade-off against Taproot. P2TR key-path spends are cheaper and more private, but for cold storage held for years the notes judge permanent key exposure the bigger risk, while P2TR remains defensible for hot wallets whose coins move often (*inference*; no hardware vendor formally recommends avoiding Taproot for this reason). Blockstream's own guidance lists P2TR outputs and reused addresses as exposed ([Blockstream](https://blockstream.com/quantum/)).

**Xpubs and descriptors should be handled as long-term secrets.** Watch-only servers, SaaS portfolio trackers, payment processors and multisig coordinators that ingest an xpub turn every non-hardened address under it into a harvest-now, crack-later target ([Chaincode](https://chaincode.com/bitcoin-post-quantum.pdf)). The notes recommend the following (*inference*):

- Self-host the indexer and coordinator (your own node with Sparrow, Specter or Nunchuk).
- Encrypt descriptor backups.
- Use a separate hardened account for each counterparty.
- Recognize that a collaborative-custody provider holding the full descriptor holds the quantum secrecy of the whole wallet ([multisig service comparison](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-multisig-service-comparison)).

**Recoverability is the second defense.** Every seed-proof design roots its proof at a BIP-32 seed or a hardened account key ([bitcoin-dev](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/Q06piCEJhkI/m/6BN8qE3gAQAJ)), so a wallet should:

- generate every coin from a BIP-39 seed on standard BIP-44/49/84/86 paths;
- record the descriptor and passphrase durably;
- detect and offer to sweep coins held on imported WIF keys, legacy non-HD keypool wallets, brainwallets or P2PK while ECDSA is still safe. Osuntokun explicitly recommends moving keypool coins into a BIP-32 wallet ([bitcoin-dev](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/Q06piCEJhkI/m/6BN8qE3gAQAJ)).

**Mempool exposure can only be narrowed.** Pay fees for next-block confirmation and avoid hours-long low-fee replace-by-fee chains. Private miner submission such as MARA Slipstream helps but "reduce[s], but [does] not eliminate, risks" and requires trusting the miner ([Chaincode](https://chaincode.com/bitcoin-post-quantum.pdf)).

**Committing a PQ leaf in a Taproot output today is not a safe shortcut, and this is the notes' own reasoning rather than a sourced statement.** Under BIP-342, a tapscript leaf that contains an OP_SUCCESSx opcode, or that uses an unknown leaf version, is **anyone-can-spend under today's rules**. A wallet that commits a "future PQ opcode" leaf therefore creates an output anyone can drain the moment they learn that leaf. A leaf that commits only to the hash of a future PQ key behind an unsatisfiable script is safe but inert, because no current proposal gives such leaves meaning. One sourced route sits beside this inference and agrees with it. Daniel Buchner noted that BIP-342 treats a non-32-byte key passed to OP_CHECKSIG as an unknown key type that validates with **any** non-empty signature. A tagged PQ key can therefore be embedded today only if it is paired with a real BIP-340 signature check in the same leaf or kept in an unrevealed leaf, with strict avoidance of key reuse, and it gains meaning only after a later soft fork enforces it ([Optech, May 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/05/01/)). Even then, the P2TR key path remains open to a CRQC until consensus disables it, which is exactly the gap Ruffing's proof assumes will be closed ([ePrint 2025/1307](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1307)). The notes conclude that **no PQ leaf can be safely committed under current rules in a way that delivers protection**, and that for today's BIP-86 key-path outputs the realistic rescue is a seed proof.

**Today's "quantum-ready" products protect devices, not coins.** The Trezor Safe 7 verifies its bootloader with SLH-DSA-128 and carries an ML-DSA-44 device certificate, and Trezor itself states that Bitcoin is not quantum-secure ([Trezor](https://trezor.io/blog/security/what-quantum-ready-crypto-security-means-and-why-it-matters)). Blockstream signed a SHRINCS transaction on Liquid in March 2026 ([Blockstream](https://blockstream.com/quantum/)). Project Eleven's Yellowpages is an off-chain registry with no consensus standing ([The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/06/19/project-eleven-raises-6-million-to-secure-digital-assets-against-quantum-threats/)). Any "quantum vulnerability" email that asks for a seed is phishing, and Lopp's guidance is "do not take action" ([ThreatBeat](https://threatbeat.com/threats/crypto-scammers-are-now-quantum-experts-targeting-bitcoin-users/)). One aggregator's "Ledger Nano X Pro" with Kyber resistance appears to be invented ([ProTrader Daily, unreliable](https://protraderdaily.com/crypto/best-bitcoin-wallet-2026)).

## The wallet stack needs new derivation, transport and hardware layers, and few are standardized

**Libraries exist but none is production-grade for Bitcoin.** `libbitcoinpqc`/`bitcoinpqc` (C core with Rust, Python, Node and WASM bindings) implements Schnorr, **ML-DSA-44 and SLH-DSA-SHA2-128s** as tapscript signature types for P2MR and ships P2MR fixtures. It describes itself as "not production-hardened". Its 0.4.0 release changed SLH-DSA parameters and dropped FN-DSA-512, **invalidating previously generated keys** ([libbitcoinpqc-bindings](https://github.com/jbride/libbitcoinpqc-bindings)). Among general libraries:

- **liboqs 0.15.0** moved to final FIPS 204 with Wycheproof tests and calls itself a prototyping library ([liboqs](https://github.com/open-quantum-safe/liboqs/releases)).
- **RustCrypto `ml-dsa`** has never been audited and has had a timing leak on secret data ([RUSTSEC-2025-0144](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2025-0144.html)) as well as a verifier that accepted repeated hint indices FIPS 204 forbids ([CVE-2026-24850](https://github.com/RustCrypto/signatures/security/advisories/GHSA-5x2r-hc65-25f9)). That is the class of bug that splits consensus between nodes.
- **noble-post-quantum** (JS) is unaudited and **does not claim constant-time execution** ([noble-post-quantum](https://github.com/paulmillr/noble-post-quantum)), so it suits verification and watch-only use better than signing.

BTQ's "Bitcoin Quantum" testnet claims a working BIP-360 implementation with ML-DSA opcodes, but it runs on a separate network and its metrics are company-reported (*secondary*, [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/03/20/btq-technologies-implements-bip-360-quantum-resistant-bitcoin-transactions-testnet/)). The research found no PQ branches in rust-bitcoin, BDK, btcd or bitcoinjs, and no public P2MR signet. That is "not found", not "does not exist".

**PQ keys can come from the existing BIP-39 seed, but only through hardened derivation.** ML-DSA key generation is fully determined by a 32-byte seed ξ ([FIPS 204](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.204.pdf)), and SLH-DSA by three n-byte seeds ([FIPS 205](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.205.pdf)). Project Eleven's draft **PQHD** spec derives them as a BIP-85 application at `m/83696968'/505153'/<coin>'/<scheme>'/<account>'/<index>'`, hardened at every level. A SHAKE256 stream feeds a scheme registry (200–202 ML-DSA, 300–311 SLH-DSA, FN-DSA reserved), and **stateful LMS/XMSS are excluded because a mnemonic cannot restore signing state** ([pqhd](https://github.com/pqdh/pqhd)). Non-hardened, xpub-style derivation is impossible for ML-DSA, whose public keys are rounded; the only proven xpub-style lattice construction uses the non-standard Raccoon-G ([ePrint 2026/380](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/380)). Hash-based schemes have no efficient public child derivation either ([bitcoin-dev](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/gOfL5ag_bDU/m/0YuwSQ29CgAJ)). The notes draw three architectural consequences (*inference*):

- Watch-only wallets must replace the xpub with a **pre-exported key pool or list of P2MR leaf hashes** that the signer tops up like a gap limit.
- Every stored key needs a **versioned scheme ID**.
- If the eventual opcode is stateful (SHRINCS), seed-only restore must fall back to the stateless path or treat the counter as unknown, because **reusing stateful counters lets an observer forge signatures** ([Optech, Sep 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/09/04/)).

**PSBT, descriptors and Miniscript have no PQ specification.** BIP-360 defines no PSBT fields or descriptor syntax ([BIP-360](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0360.mediawiki)). Builders must carry PQ public keys, signatures, leaf scripts and PQHD paths in `PSBT_IN_PROPRIETARY`/`PSBT_OUT_PROPRIETARY` fields. PSBTs with several PQ inputs run to tens of kilobytes, and an ML-DSA-44 signature plus key (~3.7 KB) already exceeds a single version-40 QR code, so air-gapped flows need animated multi-part QR (*inference*). A competing witness-v3 ML-DSA-65 draft puts a 1-in/1-out spend at **about 1,400 vB, roughly 14,000 sats at 10 sat/vB** ([Delving draft](https://mirror.b10c.me/delvingbitcoin-org/2026-04-16-bip-draft-witness-version-3-ml-dsa-65-post-quantum-key-path-spending-id2422.html)).

**Hardware wallets can sign PQ only slowly, and none does so for Bitcoin yet.** Blockstream Research's August 2026 firmware benchmarks show how slow hash-based signing is on today's devices ([Blockstream Research](https://blog.blockstream.com/hardware-wallets-post-quantum-signatures/)):

| Signing time per input | Jade Plus | Trezor Safe 3 | Ledger | BitBox02 Nova |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SLH-DSA-SHA2-128 (7,856 B) | 52.9 s | 65.3 s | 120.1 s | 100.1 s |
| SPHINCS+ 2^40 (5,776 B) | 43.3 s | 53.5 s | 95.0 s | 78.4 s |
| SHRINCS-L stateful (1,092 B) | 3.3 s | 3.0 s | 8.0 s | 5.5 s |

ML-DSA is far faster: about **3.9M cycles to sign ML-DSA-44 on a Cortex-M4**, roughly 23 ms at 168 MHz. It needs **~45 KB of stack** in the fast variant ([pqm4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mupq/pqm4/master/benchmarks.md)), however, against Ledger's ≤16 KB app-stack target. Ledger's July 2026 SDK added ML-DSA **with no side-channel or fault countermeasures in v1** ([Ledger](https://www.ledger.com/blog-post-quantum-cryptography-ledger-sdk)), and Ledger's Donjon had already extracted keys from open-source PQ code with about 40 EM traces ([Ledger Donjon](https://www.ledger.com/blog-risk-side-channel-attacks-post-quantum-cryptography)). ML-DSA signing can also take up to about 20x its average time because of rejection sampling ([ePrint 2026/1333](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1333)). Designs that sign on the main microcontroller (Trezor, BitBox, Jade) can add PQ in firmware. Ledger, which runs apps inside the secure element, depends on its vendor OS.

**Hybrid security in Bitcoin means script-level agility rather than composite signatures.** The IETF composite ML-DSA draft binds both components to a domain-separated message, but it is EUF-CMA rather than SUF-CMA ([IETF](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-lamps-pq-composite-sigs/)). Bitcoin proposals favor a P2MR tree holding a cheap Schnorr leaf alongside PQ leaves, or an AND leaf requiring both signatures, which Conduition prefers to ML-DSA-only outputs for crypto-agility ([Delving](https://mirror.b10c.me/delvingbitcoin-org/2026-04-16-bip-draft-witness-version-3-ml-dsa-65-post-quantum-key-path-spending-id2422.html)). Wuille and Conduition doubt that the BoP-2 strongly-unforgeable hybrid is worth its complexity ([Optech, Jul 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/07/03/)).

**Testing should stack vector suites with differential fuzzing.** The notes' recommended test matrix (*inference* built from sourced tooling) has six layers:

- NIST ACVP/KAT vectors and Wycheproof negative cases for the primitives ([liboqs](https://github.com/open-quantum-safe/liboqs/releases));
- BIP-360's `p2mr_construction.json` for addresses and control blocks ([BIP-360](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0360.mediawiki));
- PQHD derivation vectors ([pqhd](https://github.com/pqdh/pqhd));
- strict-encoding negative tests of the kind that would have caught CVE-2026-24850;
- cross-implementation differential fuzzing of verification (liboqs vs RustCrypto vs noble vs bitcoinpqc);
- timing-leak tests on the target microcontrollers.

## A phased roadmap ships hygiene now and keeps the PQ layer swappable

The notes' evidence supports a clear sequence. Each phase is gated by protocol events rather than dates, because no activation timeline exists. The Presidio Bitcoin readiness paper describes the ecosystem as still in its first phase and cites an emergency-activation playbook of about 6–7 months ([Presidio](https://presidiobtc.github.io/bitcoin-quantum/)). The phases and their gates are the notes' synthesis.

| Phase | Gate | Build |
|---|---|---|
| 0: Hygiene (now) | none | P2WPKH/P2WSH defaults for cold storage; enforced no-reuse with full sweeps; xpub/descriptor secrecy with a self-hosted indexer; detect and sweep non-HD, WIF and P2PK coins; standard BIP-32 paths with durable descriptor and passphrase backup; export of derivation metadata for a future prover; next-block fee policy and optional private submission |
| 1: PQ plumbing (now, on regtest) | none | PQHD-style hardened derivation with versioned scheme IDs; signer-maintained key pools in place of xpubs; proprietary PSBT fields; P2MR construction against `libbitcoinpqc` on regtest; the full test matrix; hardware feasibility tests for ML-DSA stack size and SHRINCS/SLH-DSA latency |
| 2: Target fixed | soft fork specified (output type + opcode + weight rule) | Native address type (P2MR and/or P2TRv2); per-input-type fee estimator and coin selection; multi-part QR transport; a dedicated migration-transaction flow with fast confirmation |
| 3: Migration | activation | Opt-in, then default sweeps into PQ outputs while fees allow; stateful-counter management and backup if the opcode is SHRINCS-style; Schnorr leaf for daily spends with a PQ fallback leaf |
| 4: EC shutdown | tripwire, lockdown or BIP-361-style sunset | Seed-proof prover integration for unmigrated hashed and BIP-86 coins; refuse any remaining EC-only paths |

Within that roadmap, the notes support several concrete choices:

- **Target a hash-based signature first.** Wuille, Nick, Conduition and Poinsot are pushing SHRINCS/SPHINCS+, the SHRINCS draft BIP exists, a machine-checked `libshrincs` C library exists ([Optech, Sep 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/09/04/)), and hash-based keys are small.
- **Keep ML-DSA-44 as the fast secondary leaf**, since it signs in milliseconds on a microcontroller.
- **Never ship a mainnet PQ leaf or "quantum-safe" claim before activation.** The notes' anyone-can-spend reasoning makes premature leaves dangerous, and Ethereum's EIP-7702 experience shows that a PQ path layered over a live classical key adds no security ([ethresear.ch](https://ethresear.ch/t/upgrade-any-ethereum-wallet-to-post-quantum-security-in-one-transaction-using-zk-proofs-with-a-hidden-public-key/24754)).
- **Expect opt-in features to be ignored and hardware to lag.** Algorand admitted users would have no Falcon-capable hardware at launch ([Algorand](https://algorand.co/blog/algorand-post-quantum-cryptography-roadmap)), and Solana's Winternitz vault needs a fresh address for every spend ([The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/01/04/solana-takes-a-step-toward-pqc-era-with-quantum-resistant-vault/)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. A "quantum-safe Bitcoin wallet" is not a cryptography product a developer can finish today; it is a **readiness posture whose value is decided by work done years before the fork**. The coins most likely to be lost are not those in wallets that lack PQ signing, since no wallet has it. They are the coins whose owners reused addresses, leaked xpubs, held Taproot key-path outputs in cold storage, or kept keys outside a BIP-32 tree, because those choices close off the two rescue routes the protocol is converging on: a clean migration spend and a zero-knowledge seed proof. Wuille's observation that wallet and custodian support, not fees, is the migration bottleneck ([Optech, Aug 2026](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/08/07/)) places a meaningful share of the outcome with wallet developers rather than Core.

Two open questions should shape engineering bets. First, if the consensus choice is a stateful hash-based scheme, **backup design becomes a security boundary**: seed-only restore stops being sufficient, and wallets that never modeled signing state will produce forgeable signatures. Second, the unresolved witness-pricing rule will decide whether PQ spends cost about 2x or 15x today's fees, which in turn decides whether migration runs as a slow, voluntary trickle or a congested rush after a tripwire fires. A wallet whose PQ layer is modular, with a versioned scheme ID per key, a per-input-type fee model, proprietary PSBT fields that are easy to replace, and firmware headroom for 50-second signatures, can absorb either outcome.
